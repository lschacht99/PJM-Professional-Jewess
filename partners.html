FOR WORKING PROFESSIONALS

Your experience could open a door.

A job title doesn't tell someone what the work is really like. A conversation with you can. Help Jewish young adults discover what happens behind the scenes of a profession—and what a future in it might look like.

THE KIND OF QUESTION YOU COULD ANSWER

“What does a normal day in your job actually look like?”

Or: What surprised you about your field? What did you study? What decisions do you make that nobody sees?

Your perspective. Their next possibility.

WHAT IS THE JEWISH MUTUAL?

Career exploration that goes beyond a job description.

The Jewish Mutual is developing opportunities for young Jewish adults to understand careers through people who do the work. Rather than relying only on career talks or online research, we want participants to ask genuine questions, try real-world-style tasks, and get useful professional perspective.

We're starting with young Jewish women in mind, including Orthodox women balancing career decisions with Jewish life. The experiences can serve young Jewish adults from a range of backgrounds.

01CAREER CONVERSATIONS

Let them ask what they can't find online.

Talk about your daily work, how you got there, the difficult parts, the satisfying parts, and what you wish you'd known sooner.

For example: A student interviews a nurse about training, teamwork, and the realities of working in a hospital.

02PRACTICAL EXPERIENCES

Show them how your field solves problems.

Bring a simple professional scenario, comment on a proposed solution, or explain how you would approach a challenge differently.

For example: An engineer reviews a small prototype and explains how a real design would be tested.

HOW IT WORKS

A simple first step. A commitment that fits you.

Expressing interest starts a conversation with our team. It doesn't automatically commit you to hosting an event or taking on a mentee.

  1. 01

    Tell us about your field.

    Choose as many professional areas and ways to contribute as interest you. Tell us what feels realistic with your schedule.

  2. 02

    We discuss the possibilities.

    We'll follow up to learn more and explore whether a conversation, project review, panel, or visit could be useful.

  3. 03

    Agree on the details together.

    We work out the audience, format, time, and expectations before anything is scheduled. Online participation is welcome.

  4. 04

    Share what you know.

    If we organize a suitable session, participants can ask thoughtful questions and see what professional work looks like from the inside.

WAYS TO GET INVOLVED

Choose a small contribution or something more involved. These are possibilities, not fixed roles.

A CONVERSATION

A PRACTICAL CONTRIBUTION

Explore a demonstration, panel, or supervised visit if your organization and schedule allow it.

GOOD TO KNOW

A few things professionals ask.

There isn't a one-size-fits-all commitment. The idea is to create something useful for participants and realistic for the professionals involved.

Do I need teaching or mentoring experience?

No. Being able to describe your work and answer questions is valuable. We can discuss a guided format that suits you.

Can I participate online?

Yes. We're prioritizing accessible online conversations and small experiences as we develop the network. In-person opportunities can be considered later.

What if I can only help once?

That's fine. You can offer a one-time conversation, project feedback, or another limited contribution. You don't have to sign up for ongoing mentoring.

Are sessions already scheduled?

We're collecting interest from participants and professionals and shaping sessions around what's feasible. We'll agree with you on any actual opportunity before scheduling it.

Can I contribute without involving my employer?

Yes. A conversation about your own career doesn't require organizing a workplace visit. Any workplace participation would depend on the appropriate permissions and arrangements.
