FOR WORKING PROFESSIONALS

Help someone see what your career is really like.

The Jewish Mutual is building a different kind of career exploration for young Jewish adults: honest conversations with professionals, practical experiences, and opportunities to see the work up close.

We're looking for people willing to share their perspective—not deliver a perfect lecture or sign up for years of mentoring.

WHY THIS EXISTS

Career advice should come with a clearer picture of the work.

It's difficult to choose a profession you've never seen from the inside. We want participants to meet people doing that work, ask candid questions, and explore a realistic project or problem where it makes sense.

Our starting focus includes young Jewish women, including Orthodox women who may have questions about Shabbos, holidays, workplace culture, and building a career while observing Jewish life. Professionals from different fields and Jewish backgrounds are welcome.

You don't need to represent every possible career path. Your own experience and an open conversation are valuable.

CHOOSE YOUR LEVEL OF INVOLVEMENT

Three ways to open a door.

One conversation can be useful. A practical demonstration can be even better. You decide what you're comfortable offering.

01

CONVERSATION

Tell the story behind your job title.

Join a short Q&A or career interview. Talk about a typical day, the choices you make, how you got started, what surprised you, and what you wish someone had told you.

For example: A nurse explains how a care team really works; a lawyer describes their work beyond the courtroom.

02

PRACTICAL FEEDBACK

Show them how you think.

Help shape a beginner-friendly problem, discuss participants' approaches, or review a short presentation. No need to run the entire workshop yourself.

For example: An engineer reviews a prototype; a product designer walks through why they'd change a feature.

03

WORKPLACE ACCESS

Let them see the work up close.

Where feasible, help arrange a workplace tour, demonstration, or small-group conversation. We'd coordinate the format, permissions, and practical details together.

For example: A team shows how a design becomes a product, or how a research lab organizes its work.

Interested in longer-term mentoring? That's welcome, too—but ongoing mentorship isn't required.

KEEP IT SIMPLE

What happens if you say yes?

  1. Tell us what you know.Select your professional fields and the ways you're open to helping. You can choose several.
  2. We explore a fit.We'll follow up if a conversation, challenge, or visit could suit your experience and availability.
  3. You decide before anything is scheduled.We'll discuss the audience, topic, preparation and time commitment before confirming your participation.
